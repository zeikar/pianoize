## From the cover and the band

- **The left hand still comes from the cover** where no one sings: its groove and the chords, as in the sung sections.
- **The band's parts, as long as the hand stays readable.** Take back on `L` the parts the song is known by ("Take from the band" in `arranging-rules.md`).
- **The bass line's motion.** `L`'s lowest note follows `electric_bass` where it walks, steps to a slash bass or pushes ahead of the bar line with the kick. A walk in quarter-note triplets is written at `8`, `10.667` and `13.333`, each `2.667` long.
- **A pulse replaces the left hand's figure** rather than adding to it, where "A pulse where the cover only holds" in `front-end-cover.md` gives `L` the pulse.

## How much it plays

- **A chorus is fuller than a verse** ("One velocity" in `arranging-rules.md`): a chorus can take octaves in the left hand; a verse is thinner, a single bass line and two-note chords.
- **Fewer, better notes.** Every note falls as its own bar: in the left hand, a doubled inner note adds nothing, and a fast repeated figure the eye can't follow is better as one held note.

## The limits

The check, run without `--full` as your right-hand file says, reports `notes`, `span`, `jump` and `crossing` on this hand; their limits and fixes are in "Fixing the check" in `arranging-rules.md`.

- **The left hand stays close**: a bass with its fifth, octave or tenth above, in the register below middle C.
- **The hands stay apart:** the left hand's top below the right hand's bottom at every moment.
- **Jumps take time:** keep a figure compact, or give the hand a rest to move in.
