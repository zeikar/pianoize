# An improvised solo

Where a section's lead is an improvised instrumental solo that leans on what a piano can't do, bends, slides, vibrato, long sustain, distortion, this file replaces "Keep its rhythm and its notes" under "Where no one sings" in `arranging-rules.md`, for those bars. In the dump it is a lead on a track like `distorted_guitar` that doesn't come back when its section repeats, with semitone staircases of short notes where the guitar bent. A riff, a hook or a composed line that listeners could sing keeps that rule. The solo's own line is not a band part for either hand to take, and its bars are exempt from one figure per section. Everything else in `arranging-rules.md` and your hand files holds as written.

## Don't transcribe it

A solo is the part a transcription gets least right, and the part least like a piano. Each of these has been tried on a solo and rejected by ear:

- **Note for note:** a bend or vibrato crossing a semitone comes out as a staircase of notes nobody played, and the runs sit in the accompaniment's register.
- **Note for note, an octave up:** as busy, and shrill.
- **Thinned to its contour,** graces and repeated notes dropped: what makes it a solo goes, and a plain line is left.
- **Left out,** over the section's figure: nothing on top for the whole section.

## Write arpeggios that follow it

Write a passage for the piano, on `M`, that moves as the solo moves and is made of the piano's own notes.

- **Read the solo's phrases from the dump:** where it rises, falls, peaks, rocks between two notes, breathes and lands. The arpeggios do the same, in the same places.
- **The notes are the section's chord tones,** with the colour tones its chords hold. They are not the solo's notes, except its peaks and the notes its phrases land on, which stay as the arrivals wherever they fit the chord, in any octave.
- **Let the phrases shape it.** A solo often runs over a few chords, so vary the arpeggios' span, shape and direction with the solo's phrases rather than repeating one figure a bar.
- **Give the section an arc.** The peaks climb to the last phrase, which may add one peak of your own where the solo's line sits low. The passage arrives by a step or a held note on what the next section starts with.

## Its limits

- **Nothing finer than sextuplets or sixteenths.** At one velocity a finer run sounds mechanical. A sextuplet is written in positions of two-thirds of a sixteenth, as the score's fractional positions allow.
- **The top stays near the arrangement's own:** the highest `M` note of its fullest sections, or a step above it.
- **Weight comes from register and from chords on `R`** ("One velocity" in `arranging-rules.md`): a chord under each arrival, and little on `R` between them, so the arpeggios have room.
- **The left hand keeps your left-hand file's limits.** In these bars it may change figure between phrases, growing heavier toward the end, in low octaves on the kick's rhythm.
- **A right hand written to be played** keeps its limits too: nothing finer than sixteenths, and cut a run or a chord down until the check passes.

## Recording it

Under "Inferred, filled and copied" in `notes.md`, record the bars as a passage newly written in place of the solo, how each phrase follows the solo's, and which of the solo's notes it keeps. The report names it, for the user to judge by ear.
